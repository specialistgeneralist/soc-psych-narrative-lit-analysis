**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Strategic Constructions Shaping Collective Perception and Action

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narratives are consistently theorized as purposively constructed discursive frameworks that mediate between events and their reception by audiences. Rather than neutral accounts, narratives are characterized as strategic instruments that frame perpetrators, victims, conflicts, and identities in ways that advance particular interpretive or political objectives. They operate by organizing meaning through selective emphasis, group boundary articulation, crisis framing, and proposed resolutions, thereby influencing public opinion, policy preferences, and collective mobilization. Their persuasive efficacy depends on resonance with audience values and lived experience, and they function within competitive discursive environments where counter-narratives contest meaning.

**Sub-themes:**
- Conflict framing: narratives define the causes, nature, and legitimate resolutions of conflicts, predicting attitudinal and behavioral responses
- Identity and mobilization: narratives construct ingroup/outgroup boundaries and collective identities to recruit adherents and motivate action
- Social evaluation of narratives: the perceived credibility or flaws of narratives affect the social standing of those who endorse them

**Optional note:** Excerpts span political, conflict, and social movement contexts, producing a moderately heterogeneous cluster unified by a shared constructivist-instrumentalist conception of narrative.
