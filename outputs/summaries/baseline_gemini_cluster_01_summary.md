**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Strategic Communicative Constructs for Collective Mobilization

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narrative is consistently theorized as an actively constructed discursive instrument rather than a neutral account of events. Narratives frame social reality by selectively organizing actors, causality, and group identities into coherent interpretive schemas that justify particular responses and mobilize collective action. They operate within a bidirectional communication process in which audiences actively interpret rather than passively absorb content, and their persuasive efficacy depends on resonance with audience experience, identity, and context. Narratives are further understood as contested terrain, co-existing with and responding to competing counter-narratives in ongoing struggles over meaning and legitimacy.

**Sub-themes:**
- Narrative structure and framing: identification of actors, attribution of intentions, causal sequencing, and problem definition as core constitutive mechanisms
- Persuasion and influence: narratives as tools that shape cognitive, affective, and behavioral responses in target audiences
- Identity and action alignment: narratives establish coherence between collective identity and legitimized courses of action, rendering some responses necessary and others unacceptable
- Strategic and contested deployment: narratives are instrumentally produced by organizations and political agents, evolving dynamically in response to adversarial counter-narratives

**Optional note:** Excerpts span political, media, and extremist contexts, producing some variation in emphasis between descriptive-theoretical and explicitly normative-critical framings.
