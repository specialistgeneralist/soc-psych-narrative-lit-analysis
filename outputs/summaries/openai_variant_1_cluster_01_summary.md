**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Strategic Discursive Instruments for Collective Mobilization

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narratives are consistently theorized as purposively constructed discursive frameworks that mediate between events and their social reception. They function simultaneously as interpretive structures, persuasive devices, and mobilization tools—shaping public perception of actors, conflicts, and policy options by selectively framing causes, identities, threats, and resolutions. A recurring mechanism involves the articulation of ingroup/outgroup boundaries, emotional appeals, and binary worldviews to generate collective meaning and motivate action. Narratives are portrayed as dynamic, contested, and strategically deployed by political actors, organizations, and media, operating within competitive discursive environments where counter-narratives also circulate.

**Sub-themes:**
- Conflict framing: narratives define the causes, parties, and acceptable resolutions of disputes, predicting policy attitudes and behavioral intentions
- Identity construction and boundary-marking: narratives organize collective belonging by positioning ingroups against perceived adversaries or threats
- Strategic instrumentality: narratives are deployed by agents—politicians, movements, media—to advance specific social, political, or ideological objectives rather than to represent factual totality

**Optional note:** Excerpts vary in emphasis between descriptive-theoretical accounts of narrative function and more normatively critical treatments of narratives as potentially propagandistic or manipulative.
