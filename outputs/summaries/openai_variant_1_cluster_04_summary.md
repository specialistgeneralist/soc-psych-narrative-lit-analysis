**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Significance and Action

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, a narrative is consistently theorized as an ideological meaning-framework that simultaneously performs explanatory and prescriptive functions: it interprets social reality through a particular worldview and designates specific behaviors as legitimate, necessary, or morally acceptable pathways to personal and collective significance. Narratives address psychological needs—particularly for meaning, identity, belonging, and perceived agency—by embedding individuals within a valued social cause and specifying how adherence to group-sanctioned conduct fulfills those needs. Their efficacy is contingent on validation by socially significant networks that endorse and reinforce the narrative's content, thereby transforming abstract ideological prescriptions into actionable, guilt-mitigated conduct.

**Sub-themes:**
- Narratives as significance-restoration mechanisms that reframe otherwise transgressive actions as morally permissible or laudable within a group's value system
- The social-network dependency of narrative legitimacy: shared endorsement as a precondition for psychological efficacy
- The dual explanatory-prescriptive structure of narratives, linking perceived grievances or injustices to ideologically specified remedial actions

**Optional note:** Several excerpts appear drawn from radicalization or extremism research (e.g., significance quest theory), which may constrain generalizability to narrative theory more broadly.
