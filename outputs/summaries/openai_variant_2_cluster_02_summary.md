**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Identity, Grievance, and Radicalization

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narrative is consistently theorized as a structured discursive and cognitive framework that organizes identity, legitimizes grievance, and rationalizes action—including violence. Narratives demarcate in-groups from out-groups, embed personal experience within broader ideological or cultural systems, and function persuasively to recruit adherents and sustain collective meaning. They are activated particularly under conditions of perceived threat, institutional failure, or social crisis, and operate simultaneously at psychological, intergroup, and societal levels. A recurring mechanism is the translation of diffuse dissatisfaction into coherent, action-orienting belief systems that render otherwise transgressive conduct intelligible or morally justified.

**Sub-themes:**
- Narratives as identity-construction mechanisms that establish group boundaries and threat-response frameworks
- Narratives as legitimation devices that mobilize culturally available justificatory repertoires in non-normative ways
- Narratives as psychological and emotional containers that provide ontological security, coping, and meaning under perceived crisis
- Narratives as persuasive instruments in radicalization processes, linking individual grievance to ideological prescription
