**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Significance-Seeking and Action

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, a consistent theoretical construct emerges: narratives are socially validated, ideological meaning-making frameworks that connect individual psychological needs—particularly the need for personal significance—to collective belief systems and prescribed behavioral pathways. Narratives function simultaneously as interpretive structures (explaining grievances, attributing causality, differentiating ingroups from outgroups) and as prescriptive structures (legitimizing specific actions, including violence, as appropriate means to valued ends). Their adoption is contingent on validation by a reference group, and their content shapes whether resulting behavior is prosocial or radical. This construct appears most fully elaborated within the theoretical tradition of significance quest theory and related models of radicalization.

**Sub-themes:**
- **Psychological need satisfaction:** Narratives are adopted in response to motivational deficits, particularly significance loss or threat, and offer group-validated pathways to restore or attain worth and recognition
- **Moral legitimation of action:** Narratives render otherwise unacceptable behaviors—including extreme violence—cognitively and morally acceptable by embedding them within coherent ideological justifications
- **Ingroup-outgroup structuring:** Narratives organize social reality through value-laden distinctions between groups, attributing blame to outgroups and defining collective identity and purpose
- **Social transmission and validation:** Narratives require endorsement by a relevant reference group to become operative, linking individual cognition to collective ideological systems
