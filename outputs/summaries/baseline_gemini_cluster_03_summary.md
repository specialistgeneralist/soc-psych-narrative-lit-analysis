**Proposed cluster label:** Narrative as Structured, Meaning-Making Interpretive Framework

**Synthesis paragraph:** The excerpts converge on a multi-dimensional conceptualization of narrative as a structured, causally organized account of events that simultaneously performs cognitive, affective, and social functions. Across definitions, narrative is distinguished from other discourse forms by its sequential or causal organization, character agency, conflict, and resolution. Beyond structural properties, narratives are consistently characterized as active constructors of social reality: they frame causality, assign moral valence, define group boundaries, and legitimate action. The excerpts treat narrative not merely as description but as a mechanism through which individuals and collectives interpret experience, organize grievances, and orient behavior.

**Sub-themes:**
- **Structural definition:** Narratives require minimally a sequence of causally or temporally linked events, characters, conflict, and resolution—distinguishing them from exposition or description.
- **Epistemic and framing function:** Narratives selectively organize perceived reality to define problems, attribute causality, and make particular interpretations appear coherent or natural.
- **Social and motivational function:** Narratives construct collective identity, demarcate ingroup/outgroup boundaries, generate consensus around grievances, and can catalyze action or ideological commitment.
- **Cognitive-affective engagement:** Narratives operate on recipients both cognitively and emotionally, facilitating meaning-making in ways that abstract or analytical forms alone do not.

**Optional note:** Definitions range from minimalist-structural to richly socio-political, suggesting the cluster spans foundational narrative theory and applied persuasion/radicalization research.
