**Proposed cluster label:** Narrative as Meaning-Making, Identity, and Ideological Framework

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narrative is consistently theorized as a structured, meaning-making device that operates simultaneously at individual, social, and ideological levels. At the individual level, narratives provide temporal coherence, self-continuity, and identity formation by integrating past, present, and anticipated future experience. At the social level, they are collectively negotiated, culturally transmitted, and capable of both enabling and constraining self-understanding. At the ideological level, narratives function as legitimating frameworks that rationalize action, demarcate ingroup/outgroup boundaries, and normalize particular worldviews. Intentionality and causality are recurrent structural features distinguishing narrative from other modes of reasoning.

**Sub-themes:**
- **Personal identity and self-continuity:** Narrative as an internalized, evolving life story that constructs coherent selfhood across time
- **Social and cultural embeddedness:** Narratives as collectively negotiated, culturally shaped storylines that establish normative interpretive frameworks
- **Ideological and legitimating function:** Narratives as belief systems that justify action, organize values, and structure intergroup perception—including resistance to disconfirmation
- **Emotional and ontological security:** Narratives as affective containers that manage anxiety, desire, and uncertainty through symbolic closure

**Optional note:** The cluster spans psychological, sociological, and political-ideological registers, making it genuinely multi-theme rather than reducible to a single construct.
