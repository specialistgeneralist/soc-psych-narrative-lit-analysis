**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Psychological Need Satisfaction

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, a consistent theoretical construct emerges: narratives are socially validated, ideologically structured frameworks that simultaneously describe social reality and prescribe appropriate action. They function as meaning-making systems that address fundamental psychological needs—particularly for significance, belonging, and purpose—by connecting individual identity to collective causes and culturally endorsed goals. Critically, their efficacy depends on endorsement by a relevant social network, which transforms personal interpretation into shared reality. Narratives combine descriptive accounts of how the world operates with normative prescriptions for behavior, and may include grievance structures that identify problems and assign responsibility, thereby legitimating specific courses of action.

**Sub-themes:**
- The dual descriptive-prescriptive architecture of narratives as sense-making and action-guiding structures
- Social network validation as a necessary condition for narrative legitimacy and psychological effectiveness
- The role of significance-seeking and identity construction as the primary psychological mechanisms narratives address
- Grievance-and-culprit structures within narratives as mechanisms that shape intergroup dynamics and justify particular behaviors
