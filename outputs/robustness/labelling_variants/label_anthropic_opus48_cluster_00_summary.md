**Proposed cluster label:** Narrative as Structural Framework for Meaning and Mobilization

**Synthesis paragraph:**
The cluster converges on a definitional account of *narrative* as a structured, causally organized account of events that functions as an interpretive framework for constructing meaning about social, political, or personal reality. Across excerpts, narratives are characterized by recurring structural elements—sequenced events, identified actors, attributed causality and intention, and moral or evaluative framing. They are theorized to operate cognitively and rhetorically: defining ingroup and outgroup boundaries, simplifying complex situations, assigning blame or legitimacy, and orienting understanding across past, present, and future. A consistent functional claim links narrative form to consequences—shaping perceptions, fostering shared understanding among groups, conveying values, and motivating or justifying action. Several excerpts emphasize competing or oppositional narratives within shared contexts, while others foreground personal identity continuity.

**Sub-themes:**
- **Structural/definitional features:** narratives as causally linked, plotted sequences involving agents, intentions, settings, and resolution.
- **Interpretive and sense-making function:** rendering complex phenomena intelligible and organizing perception of social reality across temporal dimensions.
- **Identity, conflict, and group boundaries:** constructing ingroup/outgroup distinctions, assigning blame, and supporting competing or oppositional accounts.
- **Persuasion, mobilization, and legitimation:** justifying action, conveying values, and influencing attitudes, including in personal identity construction.

**Optional note:** Largely coherent, though excerpts span two registers—sociopolitical/collective narratives and personal/psychological self-narratives.
