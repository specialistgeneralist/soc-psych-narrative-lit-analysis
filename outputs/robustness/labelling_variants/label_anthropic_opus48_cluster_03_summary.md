**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Significance-Justifying Frameworks in Radicalization

**Synthesis paragraph:**
The cluster converges on a common construct: narratives understood as ideological or cognitive frameworks that mediate between individual psychological needs and collective belief systems. Across excerpts, narratives are characterized as meaning-making structures that interpret experience, attribute causality for grievances, designate ingroups and outgroups, and prescribe—and morally legitimize—particular courses of action. A recurring functional claim is that narratives provide pathways to "significance," recovering or attaining personal worth contingent on validation by a reference group. The excerpts repeatedly note that narrative content can orient behavior toward radical or violent ends, or alternatively prosocial ones. Narratives are consistently framed as operating alongside psychological motivations and social network influences, bridging personal drives and shared ideology while structuring perceptions of self, others, and acceptable responses.

**Sub-themes:**
- Narratives as means-end frameworks that legitimize specific actions, including violence, as routes to significance.
- Group validation: significance and shared reality conferred through reference-group endorsement of adopted narratives.
- Interpretive function: attributing grievance and blame, demarcating ingroup/outgroup boundaries, and organizing values.
- Narratives as one component within a broader model linking psychological needs and social influence to behavior.
