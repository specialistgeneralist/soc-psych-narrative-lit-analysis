**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Strategic Frameworks Shaping Attitudes and Mobilization

**Synthesis paragraph:**
The cluster converges on narratives as constructed, communicative frameworks that interpret events and shape collective perception, attitudes, and action. Across excerpts, narratives are defined as accounts—produced by media, political agents, or organized groups—that frame actors (perpetrators, victims, ingroups, the Other), define problems and crises, and propose resolutions. A recurring claim is their capacity to transform localized incidents into shared collective experiences and to predict support for policy positions, including both conflict-resolution and aggressive measures. Several items emphasize strategic, propagandistic deployment to mobilize constituencies, exploit emotions, and articulate group boundaries, with narratives evolving against counter-narratives. Antecedents such as ingroup identity recur. One excerpt additionally treats narratives as objects of social evaluation, where perceived flaws stigmatize endorsers.

**Sub-themes:**
- Narratives as interpretive/framing devices that mediate event understanding and influence policy preferences and attitudes.
- Strategic and rhetorical deployment by groups or political agents to mobilize support, define enemies, and exploit emotion.
- Identity-based antecedents and conflict framing predicting support for violent versus non-violent outcomes.
- Social evaluation of narrative credibility and its consequences for endorsers' perceived legitimacy.
