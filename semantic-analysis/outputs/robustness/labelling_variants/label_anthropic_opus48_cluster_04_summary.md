**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Achieving Significance

**Synthesis paragraph:**
The cluster converges on a definition of "narrative" as a socially shared, ideological meaning-framework that interprets reality and prescribes action. Across excerpts, narratives perform two coupled functions: an epistemic/explanatory one—describing how the world works, ascribing meaning to existence, and supplying confidence and clarity—and a prescriptive one—legitimizing particular courses of action as valid means to valued ends. A recurring theoretical anchor is the psychological need for significance (also framed as mattering, value, control, and belonging): narratives identify culturally specific pathways to attain or restore significance and render those pathways morally acceptable, sometimes including transgressive or violent conduct. Crucially, narratives require validation by significant social networks to function. The cluster strongly reflects the significance-quest framework associated with radicalization research.

**Sub-themes:**
- Dual function: narratives as both sense-making (explanatory) and behavior-prescribing (action-guiding) structures.
- Significance and need fulfillment: narratives link conduct to mattering, value, control, and belonging.
- Moral licensing: narratives legitimize otherwise transgressive actions, reducing guilt or moral conflict.
- Social validation: efficacy depends on endorsement and reinforcement by relevant social networks.

**Optional note:** The cluster is highly coherent, drawing on a shared significance-quest/radicalization theoretical vocabulary.
