**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Justificatory Frameworks in Radicalization and Group Identity

**Synthesis paragraph:**
The cluster converges on narratives as structured, discursive frameworks that organize meaning and legitimize action, with a recurrent focus on radicalization and the justification of violence. Across excerpts, narratives function to interpret personal grievances or collective suffering, attribute blame, and frame particular responses—often extreme or contested—as necessary or legitimate. They link individual experience to broader ideological positions, drawing on existing cultural, historical, and religious repertoires while sometimes deploying them in ways that transgress mainstream norms. Narratives also construct and demarcate in-group/out-group identities, articulate crisis-solution and threat-response logics, and operate persuasively to recruit and align adherents. Several excerpts emphasize narratives as dynamic, scaling in extremity with group polarization, and as embedded within social groups that validate them.

**Sub-themes:**
- **Justification and legitimization mechanism:** Narratives rationalize grievances, violence, and norm-violating conduct by framing them as necessary responses to perceived threats or injustices.
- **Identity construction and intergroup dynamics:** Narratives demarcate in-groups and out-groups, embed shared social identity content, and link group survival to threatening others.
- **Bridging individual experience and ideology:** Narratives serve as sense-making structures connecting personal circumstances to collective or ideological worldviews.
- **Persuasion, mobilization, and dynamism:** Narratives operate rhetorically to recruit adherents and transform in extremity as groups polarize.

**Optional note:** The cluster is largely coherent but spans levels of analysis (cognitive/personal, discursive/rhetorical, intergroup/identity), with one excerpt narrowing "narrative" specifically to behavioral attitudes.
