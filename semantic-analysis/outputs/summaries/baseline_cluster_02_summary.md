**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Meaning Frameworks Guiding Significance-Seeking Behavior

**Synthesis paragraph:** The excerpts converge on a consistent theoretical construct: narratives as ideological frameworks that simultaneously explain social reality and prescribe behavior, functioning primarily to satisfy individuals' psychological need for personal significance, belonging, and meaning. Across all excerpts, narratives are characterized by dual explanatory and prescriptive dimensions—they interpret circumstances (including grievances and outgroup attribution) while legitimizing specific courses of action as morally acceptable or necessary. Their efficacy is consistently conditioned on validation by socially significant reference groups. The construct is applied most prominently to contexts of radicalization and extremism, where narratives render otherwise transgressive behaviors—including violence—psychologically accessible and morally reframed.

**Sub-themes:**
- The role of social network endorsement in conferring narrative legitimacy and motivational force
- Narrative content as a mechanism for ingroup/outgroup differentiation and grievance attribution
- The interaction between heightened significance-seeking motivational states and narrative adoption, particularly toward radical or violent orientations

**Optional note:** The excerpts draw heavily from a single theoretical tradition (Significance Quest Theory / 3N model), which may limit representativeness across broader narrative research.
