**Proposed cluster label:** Narrative as Structured Framework for Meaning-Making and Action

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narrative is consistently theorized as a structured, causally organized account that constructs interpretations of social reality, assigns identity and moral valence to actors, and orients both individual and collective behavior. Narratives function simultaneously as cognitive schemas, rhetorical instruments, and motivational frameworks—organizing perception of grievance, threat, and resolution while demarcating ingroup/outgroup boundaries. They operate across personal, political, and ideological registers, integrating temporal dimensions (past, present, future) into coherent explanatory wholes that shape sense-making, legitimize action, and transmit worldviews across audiences without requiring direct interpersonal contact.

**Sub-themes:**
- Narratives as identity-constituting structures that provide self- and group-continuity across time
- Narratives as persuasive and mobilizing tools within social, political, or ideological contexts
- Competing or oppositional narratives as reflections of divergent group interests and interpretive frameworks
- The role of audience interpretation and emotional engagement in narrative efficacy

**Optional note:** The cluster spans both psychological (personal identity) and sociopolitical (collective mobilization) registers, making it genuinely multi-domain rather than fully unified.
