**Proposed cluster label:** Narrative as Structured Framework for Meaning-Making and Social Reality

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, the dominant shared theme is a functional and structural theory of narrative. Narratives are consistently characterized as organized accounts that impose causal and temporal coherence on events, construct interpretive frameworks for social reality, define group identities and boundaries, and motivate or legitimate action. Whether applied to personal identity, political communication, or ideological mobilization, narratives are treated as cognitive and rhetorical mechanisms that simplify complexity, assign moral valence, and orient audiences across past, present, and future. The excerpts converge on narrative as an active, constructive process rather than a passive record.

**Sub-themes:**
- **Structural components:** Narratives are characterized by plot sequencing, causal linkage, protagonist-antagonist framing, and resolution elements that together enable coherent interpretation
- **Identity and group dynamics:** Narratives define ingroup/outgroup boundaries, assign collective identities, and generate shared meaning within communities or movements
- **Persuasion and behavioral influence:** Narratives function as rhetorical tools capable of shaping attitudes, legitimating responses, and motivating engagement with ideological or social positions
- **Personal and cultural integration:** At the individual level, narratives provide self-continuity and interact with broader cultural master narratives, creating potential tensions between personal experience and available storylines

**Optional note:** The cluster spans both psychological (personal identity) and sociopolitical (propaganda, conflict framing) applications of narrative theory, making it genuinely multi-domain though conceptually unified.
