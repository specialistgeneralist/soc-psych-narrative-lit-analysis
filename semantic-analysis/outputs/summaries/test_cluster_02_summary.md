**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Meaning Frameworks Guiding Significance-Seeking Behavior

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, a narrative is consistently theorized as an ideological meaning framework that simultaneously performs explanatory and prescriptive functions: it interprets social reality, defines worthy goals, and legitimizes specific behavioral pathways—including potentially violent ones—as morally acceptable means of attaining personal significance. Narratives are socially constituted structures whose efficacy depends on validation by a relevant reference group, making social embeddedness a necessary condition for their psychological potency. They address fundamental needs for meaning, belonging, identity, and perceived agency, particularly under conditions of threat or loss to personal significance.

**Sub-themes:**
- The dual explanatory-prescriptive architecture of narratives: attributing causality for grievance while prescribing sanctioned responses
- Social validation as a prerequisite for narrative effectiveness in meeting psychological needs
- Ingroup/outgroup boundary construction as a mechanism through which narratives organize values and justify differential treatment
- The motivational pathway linking significance-seeking to narrative adoption and behavioral commitment, including radicalization contexts
