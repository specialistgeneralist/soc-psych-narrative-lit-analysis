**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Strategic Frames for Perception and Mobilization

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narratives are consistently theorized as constructed, strategically deployed communicative frameworks that mediate between events and their social reception. They function by organizing meaning through selective framing—defining actors, causes, crises, and solutions—and operate instrumentally to shape public opinion, policy preferences, and collective action. Narratives are not treated as neutral accounts but as purposive constructions that advance particular interests, reinforce group identities, and structure conflict interpretation. Their effectiveness depends on resonance with audience values and lived experience, and they operate within competitive discursive environments where rival narratives contest meaning.

**Sub-themes:**
- Conflict framing: narratives define the nature, causes, and legitimate resolutions of intergroup conflicts, predicting policy support and behavioral intentions
- Identity and mobilization: narratives articulate ingroup boundaries, invoke collective emotions, and recruit constituencies around contested issues
- Social evaluation of narratives: the credibility or perceived flaw of a narrative affects the social standing and legitimacy attributed to those who endorse it

**Optional note:** The cluster is largely coherent but spans political, media, and interpersonal levels of analysis, introducing moderate heterogeneity in scope.
