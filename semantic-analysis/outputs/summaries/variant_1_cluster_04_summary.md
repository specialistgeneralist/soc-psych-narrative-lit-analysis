**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Significance and Action

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, a narrative is consistently theorized as an ideological meaning-framework that simultaneously performs explanatory and prescriptive functions: it interprets social reality through a particular worldview and designates specific behaviors as legitimate, necessary, or morally acceptable pathways to personal and collective significance. Narratives address psychological needs—particularly for meaning, identity, belonging, and perceived agency—by embedding individuals within a valued social cause and supplying criteria for what constitutes worthy action. Their motivational efficacy depends critically on endorsement by socially significant networks, which validate the narrative's content and reward conformity to its prescriptions.

**Sub-themes:**
- Significance-seeking as the core psychological mechanism narratives address, linking ideological content to individual motivational states
- The moral licensing function: narratives reframe otherwise transgressive behaviors as permissible or laudable within the group's value system
- Social network validation as a necessary condition for a narrative's legitimacy and psychological effectiveness

**Optional note:** Several excerpts implicitly orient toward radicalization and political violence contexts, suggesting the cluster may draw primarily from that literature rather than narrative theory broadly.
