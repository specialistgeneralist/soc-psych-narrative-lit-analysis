**Proposed cluster label:** Narrative as Structured Framework for Meaning-Making and Action

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narrative is consistently theorized as a structured, causally organized account that constructs interpretations of social reality, assigns identity and moral valence to actors, and orients both individual and collective behavior. Narratives function simultaneously as cognitive schemas, rhetorical instruments, and motivational frameworks—organizing perceptions of grievance, legitimacy, and agency. They operate across personal, political, and ideological registers, integrating temporal dimensions (past, present, future) into coherent explanatory wholes. Their persuasive and mobilizing capacity derives from their ability to simplify causality, define group boundaries, and render complex phenomena intelligible to diverse audiences.

**Sub-themes:**
- Narrative structure as a mechanism for defining ingroup/outgroup boundaries and attributing causality to social events
- Narratives as motivational and persuasive tools capable of shaping attitudes, legitimating action, and facilitating collective mobilization
- The interplay between personal identity narratives and broader cultural or ideological master narratives
- Narrative framing as a competitive, context-dependent process in which rival accounts contest the interpretation of shared phenomena

**Optional note:** The cluster spans psychological, political, and rhetorical traditions, making it genuinely multi-domain rather than unified by a single disciplinary framework.
