**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Radicalization and Group Identity

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narratives are consistently theorized as structured discursive and cognitive frameworks that organize grievances, construct group identities, and legitimate—potentially violent—courses of action. They operate simultaneously at personal and collective levels, connecting individual experience to broader ideological systems. A recurring mechanism involves the demarcation of in-groups and out-groups, through which threat perceptions are formalized and behavioral responses rationalized. Narratives are also characterized as dynamic: they intensify alongside group polarization, become mobilized under conditions of institutional failure, and function persuasively to recruit and retain adherents. Their explanatory and justificatory functions are treated as central to radicalization processes.

**Sub-themes:**
- Narratives as legitimation devices that frame violence or transgressive behavior as necessary or justified
- In-group/out-group boundary construction as a structural feature of ideologically charged narratives
- Narrative mobilization under conditions of social, political, or institutional crisis
- Scalar relationship between narrative extremity and degree of group radicalization or polarization
