**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Mobilization and Meaning-Making

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narratives are consistently theorized as structured, purposive accounts that perform multiple interlocking functions: constructing collective identity through in-group/out-group demarcation, framing grievances and crises to justify action (including violence), and mobilizing adherents by connecting personal experience to broader ideological or cultural systems. Narratives are not treated as passive descriptions but as active instruments—deployed by leaders, media, and movements—that shape perception, rationalize behavior, and contest meaning in social and political conflicts. Their efficacy depends on resonance with audience experience, and they operate dynamically in response to competing accounts.

**Sub-themes:**
- Identity construction and boundary-marking between in-groups and threatening out-groups
- Justificatory framing of grievance, victimhood, and violence as legitimate or necessary responses
- Strategic deployment and dynamic evolution of narratives by political, organizational, and media actors
- Psychological and social functions of narrative, including meaning-making, coping, and collective cohesion

**Optional note:** The cluster spans individual-psychological, intergroup, political, and media-analytical levels of analysis, making it functionally multi-domain despite a coherent overarching construct.
