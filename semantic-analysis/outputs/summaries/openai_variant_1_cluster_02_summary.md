**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Radicalization and Group Identity

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narratives are consistently theorized as structured discursive and cognitive frameworks that perform multiple interrelated functions: they organize individual and collective grievances into coherent ideological positions, demarcate in-group/out-group boundaries, legitimize violence or transgressive conduct, and sustain collective identity. Narratives are portrayed as both psychologically functional—providing meaning, coping, and belonging—and socially operative, mobilizing adherents and transmitting belief systems. Their content and intensity are treated as dynamic, scaling with the degree of group polarization or radicalization. The excerpts converge on narratives as the primary mechanism through which personal experience, ideology, and social context are integrated into action-orienting frameworks.

**Sub-themes:**
- Justificatory function: narratives legitimate violence or norm-transgressing behavior by invoking available cultural and ideological repertoires
- Identity construction: narratives establish group boundaries and link collective survival to the presence of threatening out-groups
- Radicalization pathway: narratives serve as the cognitive-ideological dimension through which grievances are translated into extreme attitudes and behaviors
- Dynamic extremity: narrative content and intensity co-vary with the degree of group polarization, from moderate to radical positions

**Optional note:** The excerpts span psychological, sociological, and discursive levels of analysis, making the cluster conceptually coherent but methodologically heterogeneous.
