**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Radicalization and Group Identity

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narrative is consistently theorized as a structured discursive and cognitive framework that organizes grievances, constructs group identity, and legitimizes—potentially including violence—particular courses of action. Narratives demarcate in-groups from out-groups, embed individual experiences within broader ideological or cultural systems, and function persuasively to recruit and retain adherents. They are portrayed as dynamic, scaling in extremity with group polarization, and as mobilizable under conditions of institutional failure or perceived crisis. Across personal, intergroup, and ideological levels, narratives serve simultaneously as sense-making tools, identity-constituting structures, and mechanisms of social validation within communities.

**Sub-themes:**
- Narratives as justificatory mechanisms that legitimize grievance-based or violence-endorsing conduct by invoking—yet transgressing—available cultural repertoires
- In-group/out-group demarcation as a structural feature of narratives, linking collective identity to threat perception and crisis-response frameworks
- Narrative extremity as a function of group polarization, with radicalization processes reflected in the intensification of narrative content
- The persuasive and recruitment function of narratives, operating through aspirational identity construction and social reinforcement within like-minded communities
