**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Mobilization and Meaning-Making

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narratives are consistently theorized as structured, purposive accounts that perform multiple interlocking functions: constructing collective identity through in-group/out-group demarcation, framing grievances and crises to justify action (including violence), and mobilizing adherents by connecting personal experience to broader ideological or cultural systems. Narratives are not treated as neutral descriptions but as strategic, rhetorically crafted instruments that shape perception, rationalize behavior, and contest meaning in social and political conflicts. Their efficacy depends on resonance with audience experience, and they operate dynamically in response to competing accounts.

**Sub-themes:**
- Identity construction and boundary-marking between in-groups and out-groups as a core narrative mechanism
- Justification and legitimation of violence or radical action through crisis-solution framing
- Strategic deployment and dynamic evolution of narratives by political, media, and movement actors
- Psychological and social functions of narratives, including coping, meaning-making, and collective resilience
