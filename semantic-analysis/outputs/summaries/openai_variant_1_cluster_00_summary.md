**Proposed cluster label:** Narrative as Structured Framework for Meaning-Making and Social Cognition

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narrative is consistently theorized as a structured, causally organized account that functions as an interpretive framework for constructing social reality. Recurring elements include the organization of events into coherent plots, the attribution of causality and moral valence to actors and groups, the definition of ingroup/outgroup boundaries, and the legitimation of particular courses of action. Narratives are portrayed as simultaneously cognitive tools—enabling sense-making, identity formation, and temporal self-continuity—and rhetorical instruments capable of mobilizing collective understanding, shaping attitudes, and motivating behavior across interpersonal and mediated contexts.
