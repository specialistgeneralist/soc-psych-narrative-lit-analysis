**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Ideological Frameworks for Radicalization and Group Identity

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narratives are consistently theorized as structured discursive and cognitive frameworks that organize grievances, construct group identities, and legitimate—potentially violent—action. They operate simultaneously at personal and collective levels, connecting individual experience to broader ideological systems while demarcating in-groups from threatening out-groups. Narratives are not merely descriptive accounts but persuasive instruments that mobilize adherents, provide meaning under conditions of institutional failure, and scale in extremity alongside group polarization. Their function is fundamentally sense-making: rendering contested or transgressive conduct coherent, necessary, and socially validated within particular communities.

**Sub-themes:**
- Identity construction through in-group/out-group demarcation and crisis-solution framing
- Legitimation and rationalization of violence via culturally available but unconventionally applied justificatory repertoires
- Dynamic relationship between narrative extremity and degree of group radicalization or polarization
- Psychological and social functions of narrative, including coping, belonging, and behavioral reinforcement within communities

**Optional note:** The excerpts vary in disciplinary register—spanning social psychology, political sociology, and discourse analysis—producing some conceptual heterogeneity in how "narrative" is operationalized.
