**Proposed cluster label:** Narratives as Strategic Frames for Perception and Mobilization

**Synthesis paragraph:** Across these excerpts, narratives are consistently theorized as constructed, strategically deployed communicative frameworks that shape how audiences interpret events, conflicts, and social divisions. They function simultaneously as interpretive structures (organizing meaning around identity, causation, and resolution) and as persuasive instruments (mobilizing support, rationalizing action, and influencing policy preferences). A recurring mechanism involves the articulation of ingroup/outgroup boundaries, crisis framing, and proposed solutions. Narratives are understood as socially embedded and contested—subject to counter-narratives, audience evaluation, and legitimacy judgments—rather than as neutral accounts.

**Sub-themes:**
- Conflict framing: narratives define causes, parties, and resolutions, predicting attitudes toward aggressive or conciliatory policy options
- Political and propagandistic deployment: narratives are used instrumentally by actors to advance strategic interests, deflect attention, and mobilize constituencies
- Social evaluation and stigma: narratives are assessed for credibility and coherence, with endorsement affecting the social standing of adherents

**Optional note:** The cluster is largely coherent but spans both macro-level political mobilization and micro-level interpersonal judgment, introducing moderate heterogeneity at the level of analysis.
